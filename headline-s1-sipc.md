
## Primary congressional source map — paperwork crisis and alternatives to CCS (1971)

The House *Study of the Securities Industry* (92d Congress, Subcommittee on Commerce and Finance) is a **different hearing series** from the Senate's *Securities Industry Study*. The House series has Parts 1–9; the first five concern the 1971 investigation (Parts 6–9 continue in 1972). These links point to the records, not to BASIC's missing A–RR appendices.

- **[House Part 1 — 1971 hearings (Google Books / Google Play)](https://play.google.com/store/books/details/United_States_Congress_House_Committee_on_Intersta?id=qQQ1AQAAIAAJ).** Start of the House inquiry; useful as the initial legislative context for the paperwork crisis and the clearing/transfer reforms under investigation. **Research status:** the volume is identified, but particular witnesses and printed pages have not been checked; don't attribute Lybrand, Weinberg, or TAD passages to it without a pinpoint.
- **[House Part 2 — Sept. 14, 16, and 27, 1971 (GovInfo)](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CHRG-92hhrg67228p2).** Earlier 1971 testimony, before the October–November hearings; useful for the sequence of industry and regulator positions and for testing whether objections to centralized depository processing predated the technical exhibits. **Research status:** no witness-specific TAD pinpoint verified here.
- **[House Part 3 — Oct.–Nov. 1971 (GovInfo)](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CHRG-92hhrg67228Op3).** **Eli J. Weinberg, *Taming a Paper Tiger—A System of Certificateless Stock Transfers* (May 1971), reprinted at printed pp. 1581–1585.** Compare the proposal for issuer/transfer-agent-side uncertificated transfers with the broker-centered CCS approach; this is his article reproduced as a hearing exhibit, **not** his Senate oral testimony.
- **[House Part 4 — Oct.–Nov. 1971 (GovInfo)](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CHRG-92hhrg67228Op4).** Key original industry studies: **North American Rockwell, *Securities Industry Overview Study*, printed pp. 2055–2158; Lybrand, *Paper Crisis in the Securities Industry: Causes and Cures*, printed pp. 2159–2305.** Most important volume for tracing the **pre-DTC decentralized transfer-agent-depository concept**, computerization, alternatives to central certificate immobilization, and the original studies later cited in SEC transfer-agent rulemakings. Pinpoints are the cited *printed* hearing pages rather than PDF page numbers.
- **[House Part 5 — 1971 (GovInfo; already collected)](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/CHRG-92hhrg67228Op5).** Arthur D. Little, *A Securities Handling System for the 1975 Era* (1969), **printed pp. 2560–2595**, and RAND, *Reducing Costs of Stock Transactions*, **pp. 2596–2849**. Provides competing nationwide processing/network approaches and a useful comparison against Lybrand/Rockwell and the TAD model. Cross-reference [existing research notes](comments/transfer-agent-rules/cites/support/73-study/beurocratic-ASIC.md).

**Separate 1971 Senate hearing:** [*Securities Industry Study*, 92d Cong. (Google Books)](https://books.google.com/books?id=cVb2eOtypVoC&pg=PA94). **Eli Weinberg's Sept. 30, 1971 Senate testimony begins at printed p. 94.** Useful for contemporaneous criticism of the limitations of CCS and support for certificateless issuer-record transfer. It is **not** House Part 3 and is **not** the Senate's February 1972 study *report*. See [the repository's report-versus-hearings distinction](comments/transfer-agent-rules/cites/support/72-precursor/senate-securities-industry-study-1972.md).

**Source-handling caution:** BASIC's *Interindustry Teamwork* cites/reproduces bibliographic details about the underlying studies, but the SEC Historical Society scans do **not** include the full A–RR appendices. Cite the actual congressional exhibits above where available; do not treat the BASIC appendix list as the appendix texts.

## June 1982 follow-up — nominee holding and shareholder communications

- **[Advisory Committee on Shareholder Communications, *Improving Communications Between Issuers and Beneficial Owners of Nominee Held Securities* (June 1982; full report refiled on SEC.gov)](https://www.sec.gov/comments/s7-14-10/s71410-29.pdf).** **Relevance to TAD:** This is the SEC advisory study of *issuer access and communications under street-name/nominee holding*, **not an original 1982 engineering study proposing TAD architecture**. It documents the consequence of choosing an intermediated holding structure and recommends ways for issuers to obtain information about consenting beneficial owners rather than replacing that structure with the transfer-agent-led system. It is a useful comparison with the TAD discussion in the **1976 Street Name Study** (particularly its discussion of TAD as a possible long-term clearance/settlement alternative).
- **[Exchange Act Release 34-19291, 47 FR 55491 (Dec. 10, 1982)](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1982-12-10/pdf/FR-1982-12-10.pdf).** The SEC's ensuing *Facilitating Shareholder Communications* proposal explicitly identifies the June 1982 report and the advisory committee's work; it proposed identification of consenting beneficial owners and faster broker proxy-mailing procedures. **Useful regulatory trajectory:** choosing intermediary identity-disclosure/forwarding rules over a new TAD settlement architecture. The corresponding NOBO-related changes were adopted in 1983 (Release 34-20021).

The existing [1976 Street Name Study reference map](comments/transfer-agent-rules/cites/core/75-amendments/outrefs-76.md) already links the preserved 1982 PDF and later citations. Keep distinct: **1971 original TAD architecture proposals → 1976 SEC examination of TAD in the Street Name Study → 1982 nominee-owner communications review**.
