 Under UCC § 8-102(a)(8), an entitlement order is defined as:

> "a notification communicated to a securities intermediary directing transfer or redemption of a financial asset to which the entitlement holder has a security entitlement."

That covers instructions to transfer securities from a brokerage account, including transfers to another intermediary.

But there's an important distinction for your stolen-phone example.

The official UCC comment to § 8-507, Comment 5, expressly states:

> "The term entitlement order does not refer to instructions to a broker to make trades"

The commentary explains that Article 8 governs the settlement and transfer mechanics, rather than the execution of the trade itself. It also acknowledges that a customer's instruction to sell ordinarily implies an instruction to deliver securities at settlement.

Consequently:

- Unauthorized sell order: Not itself an entitlement order.
- Unauthorized delivery of shares at settlement: May involve an entitlement order, including one implicit in the sale instruction.
- Unauthorized transfer to another brokerage: More directly falls within the definition.

The critical limitation is that § 8-507 does not automatically impose liability for an unauthorized trading decision merely because settlement subsequently occurred.

The full [official commentary to UCC § 8-507](https://code.dccouncil.gov/us/dc/council/code/sections/28:8-507) is particularly useful here.
