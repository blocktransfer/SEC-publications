## addendum — closeout discretion / Dvorak seed

Dr. Trimbath's recent point is that Exchange Act § 17A(b)(5)(C), 15 USC 78q-1(b)(5)(C), says a clearing agency *may* summarily suspend and close the accounts of a participant in default of delivery; it does not say it *must*. This is the same structural discretion targeted by 4-842 at NSCC Rules 18 and 22: Rule 18 starts with a "shall be closed out" command, then permits completion to be delayed in the Corporation's discretion for a disorderly market, while Rule 22 provides broader waiver/suspension authority.

For DEX fragmentation, plant the seed for the Dvorak metaphor around how a small drafting or interpretive choice can propagate through market plumbing. But make the LRF distinction strong: even after this **change** from *may* to *shall*, CAT identifies short trades while NSCC identifies net FTD positions; the change would alter the legal close-out duty, not create a persistent identifier that traces an individual short execution through CNS netting into a residual fail. Don't use the Dvorak metaphor to erase that distinction.
