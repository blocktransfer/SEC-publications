### rule changes

Rule 17Ad-15 — signature guarantees: final rule published at 57 FR 1095 in January 1992. The adopting material discusses requests for transfer and guarantees, but I did not find the issuance-is-not-a-transfer Q&A there.
Rule 17Ad-16 — assumption/termination of transfer-agent services: final Release 34-35039, issued December 1, 1994. This is particularly close conceptually, but the release itself does not contain the staff Q&A you're describing.
Rule 17Ad-17 — lost securityholders: the SEC's surviving 1997 webpage is here, but again, not the issuance Q&A.
1999 Form TA-2 overhaul: this was another good candidate because it asks agents to report items received for transfer. The proposal became the 2000 final rule, but the surviving rule page does not have the particular interpretation either.

I also checked the 1997 Manual of Publicly Available Telephone Interpretations / 1999 supplement route. That was a genuine SEC webpage organized as staff questions and answers, but it was a Division of Corporation Finance compilation, not the Market Regulation transfer-agent interpretations, and I could not find the transfer-agent issuance question in it.

There is an important reason the page may now be hard to locate: the SEC itself says its current Trading and Markets no-action/interpretive-letter collection generally contains letters only from January 1, 2002 forward, and that pre-2002 material may have to be obtained separately; only selected older material remains on SEC.gov.

And the distinction you remember is absolutely a real SEC staff interpretation. The surviving 1980 Q&A says that previously authorized but unissued shares are “original issue securities” and are not items received for transfer.

So my working hypothesis now is that the 1990s webpage was a Division of Market Regulation staff-interpretation/FAQ page that republished or summarized the older transfer-agent interpretations, rather than a new 1990s Commission release. That would explain all three pieces of your memory at once: 90s-looking SEC webpage + questions/answers + explicit issuance/transfer statement, while the underlying interpretation itself dates to 1980.

The exact 1990s page is the piece I haven't recovered yet. I would not conclude that you misremembered it—the SEC's surviving index is demonstrably incomplete for pre-2002 staff material.
