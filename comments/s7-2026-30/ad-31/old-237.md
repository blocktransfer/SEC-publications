**Old Rule 237** was basically a **fallback resale exemption for long-held restricted securities when Rule 144 was unavailable because the issuer wasn’t supplying current public information**.

Casey proposed it alongside the 1971 revision of Rule 144 because the SEC realized this problem: a non-affiliate could buy restricted stock, hold it for years, and still be unable to use Rule 144 because **the issuer—not the holder—failed to satisfy Rule 144(c)’s information requirement**. The SEC said it wanted to avoid “unduly restricting the liquidity” of those investments. ([GovInfo][1])

As adopted in 1972, old Rule 237 generally required that the seller:

* **not** be the issuer, an affiliate, broker, or dealer;
* have beneficially owned and fully paid for the securities for **at least five years**;
* sell only a very limited amount—originally no more than the lesser of **1% of the class or $50,000 during a 12-month period**;
* sell through a **bona fide negotiated transaction**, not through a broker/dealer; and
* file a notice with the SEC. ([GovInfo][2])

The crucial feature was that **Rule 237 did not require current public information about the issuer**. The SEC later described that as the entire point of the rule: it covered securities that could not qualify under Rule 144 because the issuer did not publish the information required by Rule 144(c). ([SEC][3])

So you can think of the original scheme as:

**Rule 144:** shorter holding period + issuer information available → ordinary resale safe harbor.

**Rule 237:** issuer won't/can't provide information + you've already been stuck holding the shares for **five years** → tiny negotiated resale permitted anyway.

It was barely used. The SEC said only **269 Form 237 notices** were filed from its April 15, 1972 effective date through April 21, 1983. In 1983 the SEC amended Rule 144 so that a **non-affiliate who had held restricted securities for three years no longer needed current public information**. That made old Rule 237 essentially redundant, so the SEC **rescinded Rule 237 and Form 237 in September 1983**. ([GovInfo][4])
