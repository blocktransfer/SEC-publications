
## 1. DTC historically could not inspect underlying customer accounts or market activity

The archived [DTC Rules](</Volumes/USB DISK/IPFS backup 2026 q1/WhyDRS/DTCC Learning/PDF Files/dtc_rules.pdf>) state on PDF page 29 that DTC's inspection authority did not extend to people using DTC through participants, their accounts, or their market activity.

This is especially significant because DTC acknowledged in its 2026 filing that the restriction could prevent it from obtaining information needed to identify and manage risks from indirect participants. The rule was consequently amended in May 2026. See [SEC Release 34-105373](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rule-filings/2026/DTC/SR-DTC-2026-005-Approval-Notice.pdf).

**Potential comment-letter argument:** Ask what risks escaped monitoring under the former rule, whether DTC performed a retrospective review, and whether the expanded authority will produce public aggregate reporting.

## 2. CNS explicitly carries settlement failures forward

The archived [NSCC Rules](</Volumes/USB DISK/IPFS backup 2026 q1/WhyDRS/DTCC Learning/PDF Files/nscc_rules.pdf>) state on PDF page 115 that unsettled long and short positions are brought forward "on a perpetual basis," netted with later activity, and carried forward as failed transactions.

Current DTCC materials still describe closing fail positions as re-netted with new transactions and open positions from prior days. See [DTCC's current CNS description](https://www.dtcc.com/clearing-and-settlement-services/equities-clearing-services/cns).

**Potential comment-letter argument:** Perpetual re-netting can obscure the age and origin of individual failed transactions. Request public CUSIP-level aging data, gross fails before netting, and disclosure of how often new trades extinguish or replace old failed obligations.

## 3. Members can prevent available securities from settling automatically

The clearest USB document is [CNS Exemptions](</Volumes/USB DISK/IPFS backup 2026 q1/WhyDRS/DTCC Learning/PDF Files/cns183_exemptions_2023Oct26.pdf>).

It states that:

- Members may direct NSCC not to settle all or part of a short position.
- A Level 1 exemption can withhold delivery even from securities presently available in the member's DTC account.
- Members maintain standing exemption instructions.
- Exempted transactions may not appear in DTC's ATP system.

The legitimate stated purpose is avoiding segregation violations and meeting other delivery needs, so exemptions should not be characterized as inherently abusive. It is nevertheless reasonable to request information about exemption volumes, duration, reason codes, controls, and surveillance.

This remains relevant: Level 2 has been discontinued, but DTCC says Level 1 exemptions remain during migration to DTC delivery authorization. See [DTCC's 2026 CNS-exemption update](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/Transformation/CNS-Exemptions.pdf).

## 4. Long customers may wait because delivery depends on another member

The 2025 NSCC filing states that receiving members have limited control over receiving shares when members with short positions do not deliver. Failed positions are then re-netted into unsettled portfolios. See [SR-NSCC-2025-013](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rule-filings/2025/NSCC/SR-NSCC-2025-013.pdf).

**Potential comment-letter argument:** Ask why investors or receiving firms must initiate a separate buy-in process instead of receiving automatic mandatory close-outs.

## 5. NSCC's rules contemplate fails lasting longer than 30 days

The archived NSCC Rules cited above charged $0.25 per CNS item failing for 1–30 days and $3 per item after 30 days. Current NSCC rules still display those processing fees.

Those fees should not be described as the entire economic penalty. NSCC now also imposes substantial risk-margin charges escalating from 5% to 100% of market value based on age.

More useful than attacking the small processing fee is NSCC's finding that extended fails may signal impaired liquidity and greater market risk. Its impact study reported that the revised charges would have reduced failure-to-deliver positions by 16.9%. See the [SEC approval order](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rule-filings/2025/NSCC/SR-NSCC-2025-013-Approval-Notice.pdf).

**Potential comment-letter argument:** Request publication of the underlying anonymized impact-study data and continuing effectiveness metrics.

## Proposed opening thesis

> DTC and NSCC's own documents show that indirect-participant visibility was historically restricted, failed obligations can persist through continuous re-netting, and members can control whether available securities are automatically delivered. The regulatory question is whether current reforms provide measurable transparency and sufficiently prompt delivery—not whether these documents prove naked short selling by themselves.
