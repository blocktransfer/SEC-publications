# Rule 17Ad-17: 18-month inactivity trigger and economic analysis

The proposal acknowledges that ordinary investors may intentionally follow a "buy and hold" or "set it and forget it" strategy, yet proposed 17 CFR 240.17Ad-17 would classify a securityholder as inactive after only 18 months without observed account activity.

The economic analysis should be pressed on whether it actually justifies that threshold. The release does not appear to quantify how many securityholders would cross the 18-month line, what share of them would still be perfectly reachable long-term investors, or how many additional notices and compliance events the 18-month choice would generate compared with a longer threshold such as 24 months. It also does not appear to compare the investor-confusion and administrative costs of those alternatives against the claimed benefit of intervening before state dormancy periods expire.

That matters because the Commission itself describes state securities dormancy periods that commonly run three years. An 18-month federal trigger therefore operates substantially earlier than escheatment itself. The comment should ask why 18 months is necessary, rather than a later trigger or a differently scheduled notice regime that could still provide meaningful warning before a three-year state deadline.

Source: SEC Release No. 34-106246, *Transfer Agent Rules*, https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/proposed/2026/34-106246.pdf.

## Broader escheatment question

Do not treat the state-law premise as inevitable. Tie this analysis to the broader WhyDRS discussion questioning whether securities held in a durable, verifiable ownership system need to be escheated at all merely because the owner is inactive. The related WhyDRS OCC comment criticizes an escheatment framework that preserves custodial control rather than giving investors direct control of their property and asks whether more law is really needed to protect entrenched custodial business models.

WhyDRS reference: https://github.com/WhyDRS/SEC-Comments/blob/d393fc3272ebaac5c53e8a4067bca8434df2024e/SR-OCC-2025-801/README.md

Potential framing: before optimizing a federal early-warning regime around state escheatment deadlines, the Commission should consider whether inactivity alone is an appropriate basis for treating directly registered securities as abandoned property where ownership remains continuously identifiable and technically reachable.
