# DTC fungible bulk, participant "ownership," and UCC 8-503

## 2019 DTC rule filing

In 2019 DTC described the legal/accounting character of participant positions in especially direct terms.

Source:

SEC Release No. 34-86897; File No. SR-DTC-2019-007 (Sept. 6, 2019), published at 84 FR 48188-48191 (Sept. 12, 2019).

SEC PDF:

https://www.sec.gov/rules/sro/dtc/2019/34-86897.pdf

Full daily Federal Register issue:

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2019-09-12/pdf/FR-2019-09-12.pdf

The relevant discussion appears at 84 FR 48188.

DTC states that deposited securities are generally registered on the issuer's books, normally maintained by a transfer agent, in the nominee name Cede & Co. It then describes the participant layer separately:

> DTC maintains Deposited Securities that are eligible for book-entry services in "fungible bulk," meaning that each Participant whose Securities of an issue have been credited to its Securities Account has a pro rata interest in DTC's entire inventory of that issue, but none of the securities on Deposit are identifiable to or "owned" by any Participant.

84 FR 48188; Release No. 34-86897 at 4.

This is a particularly useful statement because DTC distinguishes:

1. the deposited securities, registered to Cede & Co. at the issuer/transfer-agent level; from
2. the participant's credited account position, which DTC characterizes as a pro rata interest in its inventory rather than ownership of identifiable securities.

## Footnote 14: express UCC 8-503 connection

DTC's footnote 14 directly ties the fungible-bulk description to the Article 8 entitlement model.

It first cites the earlier DTC fungible-bulk description:

Securities Exchange Act Release No. 19678 (Apr. 15, 1983), 48 FR 17603, 17605 n.5 (Apr. 25, 1983).

It then cites NYUCC 8-503, Official Comment 1:

> ". . . all entitlement holders have a pro rata interest in whatever positions in that financial asset the [financial] intermediary holds"

Release No. 34-86897 at 4 n.14; 84 FR 48188 n.14.

That citation is important because DTC itself is not merely using "pro rata interest" colloquially. The filing expressly connects DTC participant positions to Article 8's entitlement-holder framework.

## Layer distinction

The filing supports the following distinction:

issuer / transfer-agent register
    -> Cede & Co. is the registered holder of the deposited securities

DTC participant account
    -> participant receives a credited position
    -> participant has a pro rata interest in DTC's aggregate inventory
    -> no particular deposited security is identifiable to or "owned" by that participant

downstream customer account
    -> another intermediary layer may create a further security entitlement in favor of the customer

This is consistent with the Article 8 concept that the entitlement holder's property interest is an interest in the intermediary's position in the financial asset, rather than title to an individually identifiable security in the pool.

## Why this matters for DRS

The distinction is useful when comparing indirect holding through DTC with direct registration.

In DRS, the investor is registered directly on the issuer's books, maintained by the transfer agent, in book-entry form.

By contrast, the 2019 DTC filing says that at the DTC participant layer the participant does not "own" an identifiable deposited security. The participant instead has a pro rata account interest in DTC's fungible inventory.

That makes the DTC account position and the issuer-recorded security legally and operationally distinct layers, even though DTC's systems may use share quantities and security identifiers to represent the participant's credited position.

## Related 1983 source

Footnote 14 points back to Release No. 34-19678, published at 48 FR 17603, 17605 n.5 (Apr. 25, 1983), as an earlier Commission description of DTC's fungible-bulk structure.

That 1983 source should be treated as the historical antecedent to the same proposition used in the 2019 filing.

## Short citation form

For the proposition that DTC participants do not own identifiable securities on deposit:

The Depository Trust Company, Notice of Filing and Immediate Effectiveness of a Proposed Rule Change to Amend the DTC Deposits Service Guide Relating to Procedures for the Deposit of Nontransferable Securities, Exchange Act Release No. 34-86897, 84 FR 48188, 48188 & n.14 (Sept. 12, 2019) (File No. SR-DTC-2019-007).

## Working characterization

A precise formulation is:

DTC holds deposited securities in fungible bulk under Cede & Co.'s registered position. A DTC participant's book-entry credit is not ownership of an identifiable security in that bulk; DTC characterizes it as a pro rata interest in its aggregate inventory and expressly cites UCC 8-503's entitlement-holder model for that proposition.
