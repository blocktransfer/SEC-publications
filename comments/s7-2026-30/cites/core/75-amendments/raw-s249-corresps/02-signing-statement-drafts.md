# S. 249 case file (2) — signing-statement drafts and technical correction

**Ford Library file:** Box 25, folder “6/4/75 S249 Securities Act Amendments of 1975 (2)”  
**PDF:** https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/sites/default/files/pdf_documents/library/document/0055/12006520.pdf

## What's in the file

This folder preserves the drafting history behind Ford's final signing statement. It contains:

- James Frey's June 3 OMB memorandum on the transfer-tax drafting error.
- Treasury Under Secretary Edward Schmults's proposed insertion for the signing statement.
- Congressional correspondence from Chairman Harley Staggers and Senator Harrison Williams concerning corrective legislation.
- A recommended presidential signing message.
- OMB's enrolled-bill analysis.
- Multiple typed and marked-up versions of the presidential statement, showing the evolution of the final language.

## Key facts

- An early recommended signing message states the policy relationship unusually directly: stronger federal oversight was being used to remove artificial barriers to competition, reduce costs, and eliminate overlapping regulatory burdens (scan p. 7).
- The same draft describes development of the national market system as a continuing task to be shaped with industry expertise rather than as a completed or centrally operated government marketplace (scan p. 7).
- The drafts connect the national market system to modern communications technology, a fully integrated trading system, broad price visibility, competition within exchanges, and competition among markets (see the repeated statement drafts beginning around scan pp. 11, 21, 28, 32, 37, 40, and 43).
- The drafts repeatedly distinguish government rule-setting from incumbent private exclusion: the proposed system was supposed to remove obsolete or overlapping restrictions and institutional barriers to entry.
- The folder shows why the final statement contains its parenthetical discussion of transfer-agent taxation: the White House learned after the enrolled-bill file had gone forward that section 28(d) contained an unintended jurisdictional effect, and congressional leaders promised corrective legislation (scan pp. 3-10).
- The final public language is already substantially present in late drafts here, but folder (4) is the cleaner source for quoting what Ford actually released.

## Key quotable passages

- Draft: “strengthens Federal oversight over the securities industry” (scan p. 7).
- Draft: “eliminate artificial barriers to competition” (scan p. 7).
- Draft: “fully-integrated trading system” (scan p. 22).
- Draft: “industry system of exclusionary rules” (scan p. 22).

## Best uses

Use this file when the interpretive question is *why* federal oversight and national-system authority were expanded. It is stronger than the final speech for showing the Administration's drafting logic, while folder (4) remains the better source for Ford's official public statement.
