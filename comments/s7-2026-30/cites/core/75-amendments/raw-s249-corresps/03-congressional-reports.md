# S. 249 case file (3) — Senate and conference reports

**Ford Library file:** Box 25, folder “6/4/75 S249 Securities Act Amendments of 1975 (3)”  
**PDF:** https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/sites/default/files/pdf_documents/library/document/0055/12006521.pdf

## What's in the file

This folder contains congressional materials used with the enrolled bill:

- The cover of **Senate Report No. 94-75**, April 14, 1975. The Ford Library scan expressly says this lengthy publication was not digitized in the folder.
- **House Report No. 94-229**, the May 19, 1975 conference report on S. 249, including the conference substitute and the joint explanatory statement of the managers.

## Key facts

- The conference report rejects the idea that the SEC itself should simply build and operate the national market system. It adopts the Senate's more flexible model under which the Commission facilitates the system while competitive forces supply much of its specific structure (scan p. 52).
- The conferees also state that competition alone might not produce essential common infrastructure. They specifically identify composite quotations and consolidated transaction reporting as areas where the SEC should use its statutory powers promptly to put integrated-market mechanisms in place (scan p. 52).
- Communications and information processors are described as the heart of the national market system. The conference materials place them under SEC jurisdiction and discuss exclusionary conduct and equal regulation in competitive terms (scan pp. 52-53).
- The conference report adopts a pervasive competitive standard for SEC and SRO rules: existing and proposed restrictions must be justified by Exchange Act purposes, and unjustified competitive disparities were to be removed (scan p. 53).
- The enacted clearing-agency provisions require rules that foster cooperation and coordination, remove impediments to a national clearance and settlement system, avoid unfair discrimination, and avoid burdens on competition that are not necessary or appropriate (statutory text around scan pp. 32-34).

## Key quotable passages

- Conferees: “national market system evolve through the interplay of competitive forces” (scan p. 52).
- Statutory clearing standard: “remove impediments to and perfect the mechanism of a national system” (scan p. 32).

## Best uses

This is the best source for separating two complementary ideas in the 1975 design: competitive/private development of market structure where competition could work, and federal intervention to require shared infrastructure or remove exclusionary restraints where competition alone would not produce an integrated national system.
