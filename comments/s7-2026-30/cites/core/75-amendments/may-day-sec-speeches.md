# May Day SEC speeches (1975)

Primary SEC speeches useful for the history of ending fixed exchange commission rates on May 1, 1975 ("May Day"). These are speeches by individual Commissioners or the Chairman; where stated in the source, the SEC disclaimer notes that the speaker's views do not necessarily represent the Commission or fellow Commissioners.

## A. A. Sommer, Jr. — "The New Breath of Competition" — May 15, 1975

**Source:** https://www.sec.gov/files/news/speech/1975/051575sommer-1.pdf

### Main points

- Describes May Day as an unusually explicit governmental termination of a long-standing exchange price-fixing system and says elements of the securities industry had tried to reverse or override the SEC's action.
- Treats competitive pricing as a deliberate retreat from rate regulation while emphasizing continued SEC monitoring for investor- or market-harming effects.
- Places May Day within a broader pro-competition restructuring of securities regulation, including the pending 1975 legislation.
- Discusses the exchange/SRO structure as historically private self-regulation that, after 1934, operated subject to federal oversight and increasingly had to justify restrictions on competition.

### Key quotable places

- **PDF p. 1:** "strong efforts by elements of the industry to cause the agency to reverse its action"
- **PDF p. 7:** the pending legislation "leans strongly toward the enhancement of competition"

## A. A. Sommer, Jr. — "Interim Reflections on Mayday" — May 27, 1975

**Source:** https://www.sec.gov/files/news/speech/1975/052775sommer.pdf

### Main points

- Calls May 1975 unusually consequential because fixed retail commissions ended at the start of the month while the 1975 securities legislation was about to be signed.
- Gives a concise history of fixed commissions from the Buttonwood system through negotiated-rate breakpoints and non-member access discounts.
- States that the SEC ordered exchanges in 1973 to eliminate fixed commissions by May 1, 1975, that the exchanges refused, and that the SEC then used Rule 19b-3.
- Explains the principal objections to unfixing rates: potentially uneconomic institutional discounts, brokerage-firm distress, and loss of research financed by bundled commissions.

### Key quotable places

- **PDF p. 9:** "The exchanges refused to do this."
- **PDF p. 11:** "The Commission never believed that Mayday and after would be 'wine and roses'"

## Ray Garrett, Jr. — "Negotiated Rates: The Initial Experience" — May 28, 1975

**Source:** https://www.sec.gov/files/news/speech/1975/052875garrett.pdf

### Main points

- Emphasizes that the SEC initially lacked comprehensive post-May-Day data and did not intend to intervene simply because firms were experiencing sharp competitive pressure.
- Identifies the inability to determine a defensible regulated price for brokerage as a major reason for abandoning fixed commissions.
- Describes deep institutional discounting as a market search for the price of execution, while warning that below-cost competition could create concentration or oligopoly concerns.
- Frames negotiated rates as an effort to unbundle brokerage so customers pay for services they actually want, while separately confronting how valuable research should be financed.

### Key quotable places

- **PDF p. 6:** "market forces would result in the right price"
- **PDF p. 7:** "customers should not have to pay for what they do not want"

## Philip A. Loomis, Jr. — "Brokerage Commissions and Research After May Day" — June 3, 1975

**Source:** https://www.sec.gov/files/news/speech/1975/060375loomis.pdf

### Main points

- Uses "May Day" expressly as the name for the arrival of competitive commission rates and reports that retail effects initially appeared modest but promising.
- Says early institutional discounting was reportedly initiated by brokerage firms seeking market share, not simply forced by institutional customers.
- Warns that firms newly setting their own prices had to understand their actual costs, something the fixed-rate structure had made less necessary.
- Says the SEC would watch the transition but was reluctant to restore fixed rates; the speech then focuses on the harder problem of paying for securities research under competitive commissions.

### Key quotable places

- **PDF p. 1:** "the dawn of May Day and competitive rates"
- **PDF p. 8:** "reluctant to ... require the industry to go through May Day all over again"
