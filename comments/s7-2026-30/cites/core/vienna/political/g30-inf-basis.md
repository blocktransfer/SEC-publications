### Named arguments and main quotations the order preserves

#### Securities Industry Association — July / Aug. 1999

The Commission identifies two SIA letters. The first addressed DTC's original Options (1)–(3); the second addressed the amendment adding Option (4). The Commission also says the SIA proposal helped cause DTC to add Option (4): a transfer agent ready to use Profile could continue adding DRS issues even if other agents were not ready.

**Argument summary:** SIA favored conditioning continued DRS expansion on use of Profile rather than letting the paper-based withdrawal process persist. It argued Profile would impose little additional systems burden on most DRS limited participants, particularly PTS users, and would create uniform electronic instructions. SIA also accused some transfer-agent representatives of reopening matters it believed the DRS Committee had already resolved.

**Main quotations preserved by the Commission:**

> “not operating in good faith”

and, describing the policy SIA advocated, allowing a Profile-ready DRS limited participant to “make additional issues eligible.”

[Commission discussion and nn.11, 18](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-09-21/pdf/99-24495.pdf#page=2)

**Date discrepancy:** n.4 lists the first SIA letter as July 15, 1999; n.11 refers to a Jerome Clair letter dated July 14, 1999.

#### Securities Transfer Association — Robert Dietz, July 14, 1999

The Commission expressly identifies the STA letter as one of the comments opposing immediate Profile implementation.

**Argument summary:** STA raised legal and liability objections to DTC's proposed Profile model and argued those issues should be resolved before implementation. The SEC says the letter addressed “perceived legal defects” in DTC's filing and “unaddressed liability risks” to issuers and transfer agents when shares moved through DTC systems.

**Main quotations preserved by the Commission:**

> “perceived legal defects”

> “unaddressed liability risks”

[Commission summary, n.19](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-09-21/pdf/99-24495.pdf#page=3)

The order does **not** identify which of the remaining anti-Profile letters made the separate arguments that Profile lacked sufficient safeguards against fraudulent transfers, that a transfer should require direct instruction from the shareholder or legal agent, or that Profile might create an invalid transfer under domestic or foreign law. Those points should remain unattributed until the underlying letters are recovered.

#### Wells Fargo & Company — Timothy J. Carlin, July 13, 1999

The Commission expressly identifies Wells Fargo as the commenter supporting a modified Option (3).

**Argument summary:** Wells Fargo supported continuing limited additions of new DRS issues while giving transfer agents more time to become Profile-capable. It wanted DTC's March 31, 2000 cutoff moved to June 30, 2000 because of the systems work required. It argued the DRS Committee needed additional time to resolve operational issues before mandatory Profile use.

**Main quotation preserved by the Commission:** June 30, 2000 was a “more reasonable date” than March 31, 2000.

[Commission discussion and n.22](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-09-21/pdf/99-24495.pdf#page=3)

### Grouped arguments that the order does not map to individual letters

The approval order gives detailed aggregate summaries but does not identify every letter within each group. Do **not** assign these positions to a specific roster entry without the original submission.

**Five primarily broker-dealer commenters — Option (1).** They generally supported DRS but said it needed a practical way for shareholders to “recover” shares into a broker account. They characterized the paper process as labor-intensive, error-prone, confusing, and slow; one reported an average “turnaround time” of 26–30 days. [64 FR 51163](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-09-21/pdf/99-24495.pdf#page=2)

**Thirteen primarily issuer / transfer-agent commenters — Option (2).** They favored continuing DRS without conditioning new issue eligibility on Profile. Their stated benefits were investor choice and issuer / transfer-agent savings from avoiding physical certificates. [64 FR 51164](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-09-21/pdf/99-24495.pdf#page=3)

Within that issuer / transfer-agent group, the order reports several distinct concerns: three opposed Profile at that time because of unresolved issues and alleged insufficient safeguards against fraudulent transfers; one objected to transfers without direct shareholder or legal-agent instruction; another raised whether Profile could result in an invalid transfer under domestic or foreign law; seven generally accepted Profile but wanted liability issues resolved first; one of those seven said Profile should not be a condition of DRS participation and issuers should choose whether to use it; and three commenters took no position on Profile but wanted the Profile debate separated from DRS eligibility. [64 FR 51164](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-09-21/pdf/99-24495.pdf#page=3)

### What the SEC ultimately did with the dispute

DTC described the STA/SIA disagreement as an **“impasse”** and said **“no industry consensus”** had emerged. After the comments and discussions with SEC staff, DTC withdrew Options (1)–(3) and retained Option (4). The Commission approved that approach on an accelerated basis. It declined to resolve the transfer agents' business-risk and liability objections in the order because participation in DRS was not mandatory, while urging issuers, transfer agents, and broker-dealers to keep working on the unresolved processing and liability issues.

[64 FR 51163–51165](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1999-09-21/pdf/99-24495.pdf#page=2)
