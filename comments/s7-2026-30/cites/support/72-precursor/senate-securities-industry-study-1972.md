# Senate Securities Industry Study (1972)

## What this is

**Official title:** *Securities Industry Study: Report of the Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs, United States Senate, Containing a Report of the Subcommittee on Securities, Together with Individual Views (for the Period Ended February 4, 1972, Pursuant to S. Res. 109).*

**Citation:** Subcommittee on Securities of the Senate Committee on Banking, Housing and Urban Affairs, 92d Cong., 2d Sess., *Report of the Securities Industry Study for the Period Ending February 4, 1972* (Comm. Print 1972).

This is the **Senate committee report / committee print**, not the witness-testimony transcript.

The underlying **Securities Industry Study hearings** are separate published hearing volumes. The later 1973 hearing in this repo on S. 2058, *Regulation of Clearing Agencies and Transfer Agents*, is also a separate proceeding that followed the 1972 study.

## How the study started

Congress authorized the Senate Subcommittee on Securities to conduct the study through **S. Res. 109 on June 21, 1971**. Senator Williams later described the study as a response to the securities industry's financial, operational, recordkeeping, and "paperwork" problems, with public hearings beginning in September and October 1971.

Official Congressional Record source:

https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1972-pt6/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1972-pt6-3-2.pdf

Relevant discussion begins around Congressional Record p. 7008 (March 6, 1972).

## Relationship to the later 1975 reforms

The 1972 report is a direct precursor in the legislative record to the later market-structure reforms.

The SEC itself later cited the Senate report separately from the hearings. For example:

- **39 FR 35214 (Sept. 30, 1974)** cites the "Report of the Securities Industry Study for the Period Ending February 4, 1972" separately from the Senate *Securities Industry Study* hearings.
  - https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1974-09-30/pdf/FR-1974-09-30.pdf
- **40 FR 7391 (Feb. 20, 1975)** quotes the Senate Subcommittee's February 4, 1972 recommendations concerning fixed commission rates as part of the history leading to competitive commissions.
  - https://tile.loc.gov/storage-services/service/ll/fedreg/fr040/fr040035/fr040035.pdf

The 1973 Senate S. 2058 hearing material in:

`../73-study/senate.md`

is therefore **not** the 1972 Securities Industry Study itself. It is later legislative testimony on the clearing-agency / depository / transfer-agent framework after the study had already been issued.

## DTC / Cede relevance

The DTC/Cede material already in this repository reproduces a direct citation to **page 14** of the 1972 Senate study:

> Subcommittee on Securities of the Senate Committee on Banking, Housing and Urban Affairs, 92d Cong. 2d Sess., *Securities Industry Study* 14 (Comm. Print 1972).

That passage describes the NYSE's Central Certificate Service, Cede & Co. as the common nominee, the absence of beneficial ownership in CCS/Cede, and the industry's effort to develop a nationwide system of certificate depositories.
