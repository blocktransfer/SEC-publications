## Main points

- The central market system should tie exchanges and OTC markets together
- A nationwide disclosure system should make prices, trading volume, and market-maker quotations broadly available, with movement toward substantially real-time consolidated transaction and quotation information.
- The Commission expressly recognized that real-time market information could require broader rules against manipulative abuses, including certain kinds of short selling.
- Competition among specialists, third-market makers, and block positioners should be encouraged, but accompanied by minimum standards and responsibilities designed to protect investors and preserve liquidity and orderly markets.
- Large institutional block trading was already a central market-structure concern. The Commission discussed block discounts and premiums, portfolio haircuts reflecting large positions, and potentially different handling of block transactions.
- The Commission planned to phase in competitively determined commission rates, including reducing the breakpoint from $500,000 to $300,000 in April 1972.
- The statement links lower transaction costs to modernization of securities transfer and clearance. It says competitive rates and a “modernized nationwide securities transfer system” could lower transaction costs, and separately points to efforts to streamline clearance “especially through reduction of the movement of paper in the stock transfer process.”
- The Commission sought to terminate reciprocal portfolio brokerage used to reward sales of investment-company shares and to restrict exchange membership arrangements whose primary purpose was rebating or recapturing commissions.
- On institutional membership, the Commission favored requiring exchange members to conduct a bona fide public brokerage business, with a predominant portion of brokerage commission business done for nonaffiliated persons. Commissioner Owens dissented in part and would have imposed a stricter separation between institutionally affiliated broker-dealers and their parent institutions.
