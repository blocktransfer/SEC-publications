# Brandeis — transparency as a basis for accountable self-regulation

## Primary reference

[Comment on SR-OCC-2024-001, *ENCORE Options Clearing Secrecy Risk Prevention*, May 18, 2024, p. 41, § III (“Self-Regulation Requires Honesty”)](https://www.sec.gov/comments/sr-occ-2024-001/srocc2024001-474471-1355754.pdf#page=41).

## Argument to retain

Page 41 invokes Justice Louis Brandeis’s observation that “sunlight is said to be the best of disinfectants” in a passage attributed to President Barack Obama about governmental openness. The comment then argues that the Options Clearing Corporation’s self-regulatory position demands **publicly checkable accountability**, not merely assurances that its internal controls and risk models are sound.

The useful principle is a distinction between *trusting an institution's representations* and *making the basis of those representations independently verifiable*. Where market infrastructure is concentrated and self-regulatory decisions affect parties without a practical competing venue, disclosure and outside scrutiny become especially important to meaningful accountability.

## Relevance to the Vienna argument

Use this as a **domestic transparency and public-oversight premise** when contrasting national self-regulatory arrangements with international clearing-and-settlement standards and their institutional development. It supports asking who can inspect, question, and verify critical market infrastructure decisions. It does **not**, by itself, establish the history, legal force, or policy rationale of the Vienna arrangements; those claims require their own primary sources.

**Source caution:** This is an argument made in a public comment submitted to the Commission, not a finding or endorsement by the Commission. The Brandeis quotation appears within the passage attributed to Obama; avoid presenting the entire passage as Brandeis’s own words.
