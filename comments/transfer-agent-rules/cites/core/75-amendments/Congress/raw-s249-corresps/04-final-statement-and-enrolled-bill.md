# S. 249 case file (4) — final White House statement and enrolled bill

**Ford Library file:** Box 25, folder “6/4/75 S249 Securities Act Amendments of 1975 (4)”  
**PDF:** https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/sites/default/files/pdf_documents/library/document/0055/12006522.pdf

## What's in the file

This is the cleanest final-public-record folder. It contains:

- The White House Press Secretary's **June 5, 1975 “Statement by the President”** on signing the Securities Acts Amendments of 1975 (scan pp. 2-4).
- The enrolled text of S. 249 immediately following the statement (beginning scan p. 5).
- The White House transmittal material associated with receipt of the enrolled bill (end of the scan).
- Exact duplicates in the physical folder were omitted from digitization, as the Ford Library notes on the cover sheet.

## Key facts

- Ford's final statement makes competition an explicit criterion for creating or abolishing market rules.
- He describes the national market system as a cooperative project of industry and the SEC, while quoting the conferees' expectation that unnecessary restrictions be removed and competitive forces be allowed to shape the system.
- The same statement links electronic communications technology to an integrated trading system in which buyers and sellers can see the full range of prices.
- Ford expressly distinguishes open competition from incumbent institutional restrictions: he discusses competition both within exchanges and among markets and calls for elimination of obsolete or overlapping regulation that unnecessarily constrains the market.
- He also treats the SEC's abolition of fixed brokerage commissions as part of a broader anti-price-regulation policy.
- The enrolled bill's purpose clause begins by directing the federal framework to remove impediments to a national market system and a national clearance-and-settlement system (scan p. 5).
- For the transfer-agent history, the final statement acknowledges the unintended state/local tax issue and says corrective legislation was being prepared (scan p. 2).

## Key quotable passages

- Ford: “competition is always a prime consideration” (scan p. 2).
- Ford: “fully-integrated trading system” (scan p. 3).
- Ford: “arbitrary institutional rules which limit competition” (scan p. 3).
- Ford: “helping to shape the National Market System” (scan p. 3).

## Best uses

Use this file for Ford's official position. It is the archival primary source corresponding in substance to the American Presidency Project transcription, but it preserves the original White House release and places it directly alongside the enrolled S. 249 text.
