Not in those exact words. That sentence is a synthesis of several parts of Ford’s June 5, 1975 signing statement.

The strongest support is this cluster:

> “This act will provide important new directives to the industry and its regulators to insure that **competition is always a prime consideration in establishing or abolishing market rules**.” :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

Then, on the national market system:

> “The key to reaching this objective will be a **new national market system for securities**.” :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

Ford immediately explains that this federally mandated national system was **not supposed to freeze the existing industry's arrangements**:

> “the national market system [should] evolve through the interplay of competitive forces, as unnecessary regulatory restrictions are removed.”  
>   
> “No Government formula nor any **industry system of exclusionary rules** can match the incentives and rewards for innovation and improved efficiency which natural competition provides.” :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

The **interoperability/integration** part of your formulation comes most directly from the next paragraph:

> “This legislation encourages greater use of available improvements in electronic and communications technology as the basis for a **fully-integrated trading system**. A system in which buyers and sellers are aware of the **full range of securities prices** will help insure that artificial restrictions on competition no longer distort the market's true expression of supply and demand.” :chatgpt-content-reference{index="3"}

And the contrast with protecting incumbent private structures is especially strong here:

> “Open competition within exchanges is just as important as competition among different markets. The right to enter these markets and provide a necessary public service should not be subject to **arbitrary institutional rules which limit competition**.” :chatgpt-content-reference{index="4"}

Finally, Ford explicitly assigns the SEC a role in shaping the system:

> “It is my hope also that the SEC will, in the process of **helping to shape the national market system**, take steps to eliminate obsolete or overlapping regulations which unnecessarily constrain the market.” :chatgpt-content-reference{index="5"}

So I would **not quote Ford as saying “central federal oversight was used to create competition and interoperability.”** That's your interpretive summary. A tighter characterization faithful to the statement would be:

**Ford described the 1975 Amendments as giving the SEC and industry a mandate to build a nationally integrated market structure in which federal regulation would remove exclusionary institutional rules and make competition—within exchanges and among markets—a governing consideration.**
