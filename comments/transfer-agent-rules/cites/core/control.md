> The securities claimed by Lincoln unquestionably would have been deemed specifically identifiable under the 1970 Act. However, amendments to SIPA enacted in 1978 (“the 1978 Act”) significantly contracted the category of securities which could be delivered outright to eligible customers. In particular, the 1978 Act replaced the category of “specifically identifiable property”, which encompassed both cash and securities, with the category of “customer name securities”, which is limited to a more circumscribed range of customers’ securities. The “single and separate fund” was changed to a fund of “customer property” which included a wider range of assets.
    > The concept of specifically identifiable property was _narrowed_ to include only those securities registered (or in the process of being registered) in the customer’s name (“customer name securities”). The single and separate fund was correspondingly broadened to include available cash and all fully paid-for securities and margin securities (“customer property”).
