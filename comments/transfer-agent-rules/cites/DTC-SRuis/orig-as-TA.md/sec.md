**Yes. I found the original 1972 SEC filing notice confirming that CCS proposed to prohibit itself from acting as a transfer agent.** I also found a detailed history identifying the SEC correspondence that should help establish when and why the prohibition was deleted.

The distinction is that **the prohibition itself is documented; the SEC's alleged demand for its deletion still needs the underlying correspondence.**

## 1. November 8, 1972 — The original proposed prohibition

**SEC Exchange Act Release No. 34-9849**  
*Central Certificate Service, Inc.—Proposals Released for Public Comment*  
Published November 21, 1972, at **37 FR 24795–24797**.

[Read the original Federal Register PDF](https://archives.federalregister.gov/issue_slice/1972/11/21/24794-24798.pdf)

On **page 24795, right column**, under *Certificate of Incorporation of CCS, Inc.*, the SEC states:

> “The new charter, as submitted, specifically precludes CCS, Inc., from acting as a transfer agent.”

That's direct, contemporaneous confirmation that the restriction existed in the proposal. [Federal Register Archives](https://archives.federalregister.gov/issue_slice/1972/11/21/24794-24798.pdf)

Importantly, the SEC identifies this as a provision of CCS's **proposed corporate charter**, not merely an operating rule.

The same release identifies the Banking and Securities Industry Committee (BASIC), whose sponsors included the NYSE, American Stock Exchange, NASD, and **11 New York Clearing House banks**. That documents the banks' institutional involvement, although it doesn't establish that they specifically demanded the prohibition. [Federal Register Archives](https://archives.federalregister.gov/issue_slice/1972/11/21/24794-24798.pdf)

## 2. The SEC's review and the correspondence surrounding it

I also found an unusually useful historical account:

**BASIC: Interindustry Teamwork (1984)**

[Read the historical BASIC report (PDF)](https://www.sechistorical.org/collection/papers/1980/1984_0401_BasicTeamwork_1.pdf)

Its discussion of CCS and DTC's formation, particularly **printed pages 38–43**, gives a detailed chronology:

| Date | Document or event |
|---|---|
| May 12, 1972 | BASIC task force paper considering whether the depository could become a co-transfer agent |
| September 15, 1972 | Proposed charter, bylaws, and rules submitted to the SEC |
| September 29, 1972 | CCS personnel met with SEC staff to discuss the submission |
| October 18, 1972 | Revised papers submitted to the SEC |
| November 8, 1972 | SEC Release 34-9849 publicly announced the proposal |
| May 8, 1973 | SEC clearance letter identifying changes required to DTC's rules and other legal papers |
| May 21, 1973 | DTC chairman William Dentzer appeared before the Commission |
| July 12, 1973 | SEC letter confirming resolution of a disputed participation requirement |

The report specifically says that the **May 8, 1973 SEC letter required certain changes to DTC's rules and other legal documents**. It discusses a dispute over broker-dealer participation, but does not identify the transfer-agent prohibition as one of those changes. [SEC Historical Society](https://www.sechistorical.org/collection/papers/1980/1984_0401_BasicTeamwork_1.pdf)

This makes the May 8 correspondence an important lead, rather than proof of the deletion.

There is another interesting complication: the BASIC history says its task force independently considered making the depository a **co-transfer agent** in May 1972, but rejected the idea because of procedural complexity, risk, and doubts about processing speed. That provides an alternative documented explanation for opposition to combining those functions, though it does not disprove the account of bank pressure. [SEC Historical Society](https://www.sechistorical.org/collection/papers/1980/1984_0401_BasicTeamwork_1.pdf)

## 3. February 21, 1974 — The SEC chairman explicitly proposes combining the functions

**SEC News Digest, February 22, 1974, page 1**

[Read the original SEC News Digest (PDF)](https://www.sec.gov/news/digest/1974/dig022274.pdf)

The SEC reported that Chairman Ray Garrett Jr. had identified several potential improvements to securities processing.

His third proposal was:

> “Have the depository function as a transfer agent.”

He explained that combining the functions could reduce communication difficulties and certificate issuance costs. [Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/news/digest/1974/dig022274.pdf)

That is particularly significant because it establishes that, by February 1974, the SEC chairman was publicly discussing precisely the arrangement CCS's proposed November 1972 charter would have prohibited.

It does **not**, by itself, establish that the Commission had compelled CCS to remove the provision.

### What we can establish now

**Confirmed:** In November 1972, CCS submitted a proposed corporate charter that expressly prohibited CCS from acting as a transfer agent.

**Confirmed:** The proposal emerged from an institutional process involving major New York banks, and the SEC subsequently required changes to DTC's organizational documents.

**Confirmed:** By February 1974, the SEC chairman publicly identified having a depository act as a transfer agent as a potential improvement.

**Not yet independently confirmed:** That the banks specifically forced CCS to include the prohibition, and that the SEC specifically demanded its deletion.

**The strongest next document is the May 8, 1973 SEC clearance letter.** Together with the original and final charters, it should allow a direct comparison of what CCS proposed, what the SEC required, and what ultimately took effect.
