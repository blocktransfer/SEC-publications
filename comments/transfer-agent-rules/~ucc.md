## 2. 1981 Buffett — stock in your own name

Warren E. Buffett, *Chairman's Letter — 1981*, Berkshire Hathaway Inc. (Feb. 26, 1982), https://www.berkshirehathaway.com/letters/1981.html.

> “be sure to have your stock registered in your name well before September 30th”

Buffett gives the advice while describing Berkshire's 1981 shareholder-designated charitable-contribution program. Shares held in nominee names such as brokers were not eligible under the Treasury ruling, and Berkshire also encountered material delays when brokers forwarded communications to beneficial owners. Buffett therefore recommended direct registration for shareholders who wanted to participate and suggested that even shareholders who otherwise preferred nominee ownership keep at least one share registered in their own name so they would receive important corporate news at the same time as other shareholders.

### Addendum

After formal IRS clarification, Buffett explains that a property law "did not cover participation by shareholders whose stock was registered in the name of nominees, such as brokers, and additionally required that the owners" certify ownership of their own assets.

Fitzgerald v. Circle Internet Financial
U.S. District Court, District of Massachusetts · August 25, 2026 · 1:25-cv-12561

This is an even closer match to an actual cyberattack.

The plaintiff alleged that an attacker gained access to her Crypto.com wallet in August 2021 and stole 81,147 USDC. The stolen tokens were later moved to another address.

The plaintiff asked Circle to freeze the stolen assets and restore their value.

The record establishes that Circle did place an access denial on the destination wallet following a New Hampshire state-court order. The plaintiff subsequently sought replacement tokens through litigation.

The federal court reached two different conclusions:
- Replevin: Dismissed. Circle did not possess or wrongfully detain the actual tokens, and issuing equivalent tokens would not constitute returning the specific property.
- Equitable restitution and constructive trust: Allowed to proceed because the plaintiff plausibly alleged that Circle benefited from reserves supporting tokens that could no longer be redeemed.
This was a motion-to-dismiss ruling, not a damages judgment.

6. Harris v. TD Ameritrade
805 F.3d 664 (6th Cir. 2015)https://www.courtlistener.com/opinion/3008690/muriel-harris-v-td-ameritrade-inc/
Read the Sixth Circuit opinionThis is especially relevant to direct registration and transfer-agent modernization.

Investors sought to move street-name shares into direct ownership, but TD Ameritrade refused because DTC had imposed a global lock following the creation of hundreds of millions of invalid shares.

The investors invoked federal customer-protection rules and UCC Article 8, including § 8-508.

Result: The Sixth Circuit affirmed dismissal, concluding that the provisions invoked did not provide the private cause of action necessary to compel the requested change in holding form.

Importance: A statutory duty does not necessarily mean that an investor can independently sue to enforce it under that provision. This holding concerning §§ 8-504, 8-506, 8-507(a), and 8-508 should not be confused with § 8-507(b), which expressly provides a restoration-and-damages remedy

Ventura v. Circle Internet Financial
Delaware Superior Court · August 18 and October 7, 2025 · N25C-01-109 CLS

This is a particularly interesting application of UCC Article 8 to digital assets.

A plaintiff attempted to transfer 511,435 USDC to an Ethereum address. The tokens were accidentally sent to a different address, differing by one character, and reportedly became inaccessible.

The plaintiff sued Circle for replacement tokens or their equivalent value, asserting that UCC § 8-405's replacement remedy could apply.

Circle argued that USDC was not an Article 8 security and that blockchain tokens could not be certificated securities.

The court denied Circle's motion to dismiss, finding that further factual development was needed to determine whether a digital token could satisfy Article 8's definitions. On October 7, 2025, it also denied Circle's motion for reargument.

This was not a final finding that USDC is a security or that Circle owes reimbursement. It was a ruling allowing the claim to proceed.

Read the Georgia Court of Appeals opinionThis case involved securities subject to transfer restrictions that were not properly disclosed on the certificates. A broker-dealer alleged that it relied on information from SunTrust, acting as transfer agent, and suffered losses when dealing with the shares.

The appellate court reversed dismissal of the claims, allowing the broker-dealer's theories concerning the transfer agent's representations and statutory obligations to proceed.

Why it matters: Transfer-agent conduct can create claims involving people other than the issuer and the registered shareholder.
