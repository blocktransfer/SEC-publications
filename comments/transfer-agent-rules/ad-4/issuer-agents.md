Under Rule 17Ad-4(b), a transfer agent qualified as an exempt transfer agent if, across any six consecutive months, it received:

- Fewer than 500 items for transfer.
- Fewer than 500 items for processing.

The exemption relieved qualifying transfer agents from certain turnaround, recordkeeping, and retention requirements.

Previously, qualifying agents had to file an exemption notice with their appropriate regulatory agency (ARA) within 10 business days after the qualifying six-month period.

The amendment established different requirements depending on the ARA:

| Regulator       | Before 1983 | After amendment               |
| --------------- | ----------- | ----------------------------- |
| SEC             | File notice | Prepare and retain internally |
| OCC             | File notice | Prepare and retain internally |
| Federal Reserve | File notice | Still file notice             |
| FDIC            | File notice | Still file notice             |



Source: 48 FR 28245 (June 21, 1983), Section II, Deletion of the Notice Requirements—Rule 17Ad-4(b) and (c).

> "In the October Release, the Commission proposed to eliminate the requirement that, under appropriate circumstances, a registered transfer agent file an Exemption Notice with its ARA."

The first source explicitly confirms that the original proposal was broader than the rule ultimately adopted.

The SEC originally proposed eliminating the filing obligation more broadly.

The Federal Reserve and FDIC opposed the change because they used exemption notices in their examinations and supervisory responsibilities. The OCC supported eliminating the filing requirement.

The SEC accommodated the Federal Reserve and FDIC by preserving their filing requirements.

The important distinction: SEC- and OCC-regulated agents did not lose their exemption, nor were they relieved of documenting it. They simply no longer had to submit the notice to their regulator.
