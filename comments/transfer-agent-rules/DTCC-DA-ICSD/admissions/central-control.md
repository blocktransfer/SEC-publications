### 1. DTCC centrally manages the network
> “manages the SMART network”
Securely Managed and Reliable Technology.
It's DTCC's privately managed telecommunications network connecting banks, broker-dealers, and other participants to DTCC's clearing and settlement infrastructure.

**86 FR 57222 — Section II.A, Background**

The SEC explains that DTCC operates the SMART network connecting DTC participants to a nationwide infrastructure of networks, processing centers, and control facilities.

This establishes centralized management of the technical infrastructure.

### 2. DTC can unilaterally disconnect participants
> “DTC would be able to disconnect a participant from its network”

**86 FR 57223 — Section II.B.3, Systems Disconnect Rule**

Rule 38A authorizes DTC to disconnect participants when qualifying systems disruptions threaten its operations.

The authority does not require the disconnected participant's consent.

### 3. DTC can suspend communications
> “suspending data transmissions between DTC and the participant”

**86 FR 57224 — Section III.A, Commission Findings**

The SEC expressly acknowledges DTC's authority to interrupt electronic communications and require affected participants to take protective actions.

### 4. Emergency authority is concentrated in management

**86 FR 57223 — Section II.B.3**

A designated DTC officer can initially determine that a Major Event exists. A management committee then convenes, and the Board must review the action as soon as practicable, within five business days.

**The important distinction:** DTC does not need advance Board approval to initiate a Rule 38A response.

### 5. Rule 38A takes precedence over conflicting DTC rules

**86 FR 57223 — Section II.B.3**

The approved framework provides that Rule 38A prevails when its provisions conflict with other DTC rules or procedures.

This reinforces DTC's centralized emergency authority.

 **Yes. DTC exercises centralized control over transfer agents' connections to FAST and DRS, including the authority to terminate their participation.** There is actually an SEC approval order specifically addressing this.

### 1. DTC can terminate a transfer agent's FAST connection

**SEC Release 34-60196 — 74 FR 33496 (July 13, 2009)**

On page 10 of the SEC order, DTC is granted authority:

> “using its sole discretion, to terminate or to continue the transfer agent’s status as a FAST agent.”

This provision concerns existing agents that failed to satisfy the approved requirements within the specified six-month transition period.

The same authority appears on **page 11**, concerning DRS Limited Participants. [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules/sro/dtc/2009/34-60196.pdf)

### 2. DTC controls technical requirements for transfer-agent connectivity

The order establishes that transfer agents must:

- Maintain electronic communications with DTC for daily FAST reconciliation.
- Execute DTC's agreements and follow its operational criteria.
- Implement changes required by modifications to DTC's internal systems.
- Participate in DTC's required training and insurance arrangements.

These requirements give DTC substantial control over the technical and contractual conditions of participation.

**Pages 8–11 of the SEC order.** [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules/sro/dtc/2009/34-60196.pdf)

### 3. Transfer agents actually challenged this centralized power

This is particularly interesting.

In August 2009, the **Securities Transfer Association (STA)** petitioned the SEC to reconsider its approval.

The STA argued that DTC was attempting to become:

> “the de facto regulator of the transfer agent industry”

It argued that transfer agents lacked meaningful participation in DTC governance while being subjected to requirements affecting their ability to serve public companies.

**STA Petition for Commission Review, August 4, 2009, pages 3–5.** [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules/other/2009/stapetition080409.pdf)

The SEC's approval order rejected the argument that DTC was improperly regulating transfer agents, reasoning that the requirements applied to agents choosing to participate in FAST and DRS. [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/rules/sro/dtc/2009/34-60196.pdf)

### 4. This extends to the actual transfer-agent interface

DTC's modern Securities Processing Application (SPA) provides centralized access to FAST balance confirmations, DWAC deposits and withdrawals, and DRS transactions.

Access is provisioned through DTCC's portal and its authorization system.

A **September 30, 2026 proposal, SR-DTC-2026-012**, would further modernize these connections using SPA, APIs, and messaging interfaces. That filing is a proposal, not yet an approved rule. ### The distinction that matters

**DTC does not centrally control transfer agents' independent shareholder registers.**

However, it controls the infrastructure through which participating transfer agents interact electronically with the depository.

Consequently, terminating FAST or DRS participation can interrupt an agent's ability to process transfers through those systems, even though the agent continues maintaining its issuer's shareholder register.

That's a stronger demonstration of centralized control over the **interface between registered ownership and depository ownership** than Rule 38A alone.
