### context 

there are separate banking-agency regulatory provisions governing where/how Form TA-1 is filed, but not genuinely separate substantive TA-1 regimes. Form TA-1 was deliberately developed as a uniform interagency form by the SEC and banking agencies in 1975. [GovInfo](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2017-07-25/pdf/FR-2017-07-25.pdf)

| ARA | Banking-agency provision for TA-1 | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| OCC | 12 CFR § 9.20(a)(1)-(2) | Requires an OCC-regulated transfer agent to file FFIEC Form TA-1 with the OCC; amendments are due within 60 days after information becomes inaccurate, misleading, or incomplete. [Legal Information Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/9.20) |
| Federal Reserve | 12 CFR § 208.31(a) (Regulation H) | Instead of creating its own registration regime, the Board expressly makes SEC Rule 17Ac2-1 applicable to state-member-bank transfer agents, with references to the "Commission" treated as references to the Board. [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/regulations/section-20831-state-member-banks-as-transfer-agents.htm) |
| Federal Reserve—BHCs, etc. | 12 CFR § 225.4(d) (Regulation Y) | Makes §§ 208.31–208.33 applicable to covered BHCs and bank subsidiaries as though they were state member banks. [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/regulations/section-2254-corporate-practices.htm) |
| FDIC | 12 CFR §§ 341.3–341.4 | Requires FDIC-regulated TAs to file Form TA-1 with the FDIC and amendments within 60 days. [Legal Information Institute](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/12/341.3) |
| OTS (historical) | No comparable TA-1 regulation | This is important: the FDIC expressly states that OTS never issued a regulation comparable to FDIC Part 341. OTS became an ARA and instructed covered thrifts to register, but implemented that obligation through guidance and the uniform interagency form rather than its own comprehensive regulation. [FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/news/board-matters/2016/2016-04-26-notice-sum-b-mem.pdf) |

The Federal Reserve provision is particularly revealing. Section 208.31(a) literally says that the SEC's registration procedures in 17 CFR § 240.17Ac2-1 apply to member-bank transfer agents, except that "Commission" means the Federal Reserve Board. Section 208.31(b) then does essentially the same thing with the SEC's operational/reporting rules, 17 CFR §§ 240.17Ac2-2 and 240.17Ad-1 through 17Ad-16. [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/frrs/regulations/section-20831-state-member-banks-as-transfer-agents.htm)

And the form itself confirms this structure. The historical FFIEC TA-1 had a single checkbox asking which ARA applied—OCC, FDIC, Federal Reserve, or OTS—and otherwise used the same form. [FFIEC](https://www.ffiec.gov/PDF/FFIEC_forms/TA1-FORM.pdf) The current OCC instructions likewise say that each registrant files TA-1 with its own ARA and that the federal banking regulators send copies of the filings to the SEC. [OCC](https://www.occ.treas.gov/publications-and-resources/forms/form-ta-1-instructions.pdf)

So the short answer for your larger research question is:

> There are separate ARA filing channels, but that should not be confused with separate substantive transfer-agent registration regimes.

The OCC, Fed, and FDIC each have a regulatory hook telling their institutions to register with _them_. But the underlying TA-1 is an interagency form, and in the Federal Reserve's case the governing regulation expressly imports the SEC's own registration rule. Historically OTS is an even stronger example: it was an ARA but never promulgated a comparable TA-registration regulation at all. [FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/news/board-matters/2016/2016-04-26-notice-sum-b-mem.pdf)

That distinction is potentially quite important to the historical "duplicative SEC oversight" argument: being assigned a banking ARA did not necessarily mean Congress or the banking agency created an independent, parallel body of transfer-agent regulation.
