
### 1. SEC Commissioner John R. Evans — October 9, 1975 (strongest source)

**Document:** *Transfer Agents Meet the SEC*, p. 4.

Evans explained:

> The uniform proposed form for the registration of all transfer agents was developed through consultation and cooperation between the SEC and bank regulators

He continued that the objective was:

> to establish comparable requirements for bank and non-bank transfer agents.

This is nearly an exact statement of your claim. [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/files/news/speech/1975/100975evans.pdf)

### 2. SEC Annual Report — 1976

**Document:** *42nd Annual Report of the Securities and Exchange Commission*, section titled *Registration and Regulation of Transfer Agents*.

The SEC reported that, in October 1975, it adopted Rule 17Ac2-1 and Form TA-1 in cooperation with the federal banking agencies.

It specifically stated:

> The three bank regulatory agencies simultaneously adopted a similar rule and the identical registration form

This independently establishes that the agencies adopted the **same registration form**, although their implementing rules were not necessarily identical. [SEC](https://www.sec.gov/files/about/annual_report/1976.pdf)

**Primary source:** [SEC Annual Report, 1976 (PDF)](https://www.sec.gov/files/about/annual_report/1976.pdf)

### 3. Federal Reserve — Historical confirmation

The Federal Reserve's official Form TA-1 documentation states:

> The interagency reporting form was developed and implemented in 1975.

It identifies the form as an interagency registration instrument and explains its statutory basis under Section 17A(c) of the Exchange Act. [Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/apps/reportingforms/Report/Index/Form_TA-1)

**Primary source:** [Federal Reserve — Form TA-1 Reporting Forms](https://www.federalreserve.gov/apps/reportingforms/Report/Index/Form_TA-1)
