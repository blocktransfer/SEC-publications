## 1. DTC Rule 4(A): The authority to pledge participants' securities

**Primary source:** [DTC Rules, Rule 4(A), printed pages 50–51](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rules/dtc_rules.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com#page=54)

The current rules, dated April 28, 2026, contain a provision entitled:

**RULE 4(A) — PLEDGE OF PROPERTY TO THE CORPORATION AND ITS LENDERS**

Section 1 expressly authorizes DTC to pledge, repledge, hypothecate, transfer, or assign three categories of collateral:

1. Participants Fund cash and its investments.
2. **Net Additions, including participants' security entitlements in those securities.**
3. DTC preferred stock held by participants.

Remarkably, the rule permits loans that exceed the participant obligations secured by the pledged property and remain outstanding longer than those obligations.

It also provides that participants cannot demand their collateral directly from the secured lender while the relevant obligations remain unpaid. [DTCC](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rules/dtc_rules.pdf)

### The provision that establishes the chain

**Rule 4(A), Section 2, printed page 50**, expressly describes the transfer of a security interest:

**Participant → DTC → Lender**

It specifies that DTC makes book entries reflecting the security interest passing from the participant to DTC and then from DTC to the lender.

This is considerably stronger evidence than merely pointing to Cede's registered ownership. The rule provides an express contractual mechanism for repledging participants' interests. [DTCC](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rules/dtc_rules.pdf)

## 2. Rule 9(B): When DTC can use those securities

**Primary source:** [DTC Rule 9(B), Section 2, printed pages 66–67](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rules/dtc_rules.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com#page=70)

This rule expressly addresses a participant's failure to settle.

If the relevant default conditions occur, DTC may pledge a defaulting participant's Net Additions to secure a loan to DTC.

Importantly, the rule permits DTC to pledge or sell Net Additions even when the proceeds exceed the participant's negative settlement balance.

That establishes a documented mechanism through which securities credited to a participant's account can support DTC's borrowing obligations. [DTCC](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rules/dtc_rules.pdf)

Not every security held through DTC is a Net Addition. Rule 1 defines the category, and securities cease to qualify when they become segregated or pledged securities, among other circumstances.

## 3. Federal Reserve Operating Circular 10: How the Fed obtains its lien

**Primary source:** [Federal Reserve Operating Circular 10, Section 6.0, PDF page 9](https://www.frbservices.org/binaries/content/assets/crsocms/resources/rules-regulations/082823-operating-circular-10.pdf?utm_source=chatgpt.com#page=9)

This is the other essential document.

Section 6.0 is entitled **GRANT OF SECURITY INTEREST**.

It provides that, in consideration of receiving borrowing privileges, the borrower grants the lending Federal Reserve Bank a continuing security interest and lien in the designated collateral.

Section 7 addresses collateral maintenance and perfection.

Section 9.1(d) requires the borrower to represent that it possesses sufficient rights in the collateral to grant an enforceable security interest.

Consequently, assuming DTC obtained emergency borrowing privileges under 12 USC 5465(b) and entered into an applicable lending agreement, the legal structure could be:

| Stage | Legal mechanism |
|---|---|
| Participant → DTC | DTC Rule 4(A), supported by participant agreements |
| DTC → Federal Reserve Bank | Loan security agreement, potentially Operating Circular 10 |
| Attachment | NY UCC 9-203 |
| Perfection | NY UCC Article 9, potentially through control or filing |
| Priority in a securities shortfall | NY UCC 8-511(c), if its conditions are satisfied |

**Title VIII authorizes the emergency lending. It does not itself grant the Fed a lien.** The lien would arise through an enforceable security arrangement. [Federal Reserve Financial Services](https://www.frbservices.org/binaries/content/assets/crsocms/resources/rules-regulations/082823-operating-circular-10.pdf)

## 4. Does the participant's agreement satisfy NY UCC 8-504(b)?

This is the next important legal question.

NY UCC 8-504(b) restricts securities intermediaries from granting security interests in assets maintained for entitlement holders, except as otherwise agreed by those entitlement holders.

Two findings are particularly relevant.

**First**, DTC Rule 1 identifies participants as the entitlement holders of securities credited to their DTC accounts.

**Second**, the SEC's 2021 release 34-92249 confirms that DTC's Participant Agreement binds participants to its rules and procedures, including pledge-related provisions. [DTCC](https://www.dtcc.com/-/media/Files/Downloads/legal/rules/dtc_rules.pdf)

Together, these support an argument that participants have contractually agreed to DTC's Rule 4(A) collateral arrangements.

However, establishing that agreement's legal effect under 8-504(b) still requires examining the participant agreement and applicable limitations.

**Importantly, the participant's consent is not necessarily the underlying investor's individual consent.** The investor may hold an entitlement against a broker, while the broker holds the corresponding entitlement against DTC.

That distinction is central to the customer-property question.

## 5. What evidence is still missing?

We can now establish DTC's contractual authority to repledge qualifying securities and identify how the Federal Reserve ordinarily receives collateral rights.

But to establish an **actual Federal Reserve security interest in particular DTC-held securities**, I would want three additional records:

| Evidence | What it establishes |
|---|---|
| DTC–Federal Reserve executed lending agreement | Whether DTC granted the Reserve Bank a lien under an actual borrowing arrangement |
| Applicable collateral schedule and DTC pledge records | Precisely which assets were pledged and when |
| Control agreement, financing statements, or equivalent perfection records | Whether and how the Reserve Bank's interest was perfected |

Those transaction-specific records are not established by the public rules alone.

There is an especially useful SEC precedent here: **Release 34-79088 (2016)** explains that DTC book-entry pledging facilities do not independently establish every element of an enforceable security interest. The agreements between the pledgor and pledgee must satisfy applicable law. [Securities and Exchange Commission](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/dtc/2016/34-79088.pdf)

## 6. The consequence under NY UCC 8-511(c)

Once an enforceable security interest exists, NY UCC 8-511(c) becomes especially important.

For a clearing corporation such as DTC, the provision gives a secured creditor priority over entitlement holders when the clearing corporation lacks sufficient financial assets to satisfy both sets of claims.

That rule doesn't independently create the security interest. **But it can determine who has priority after the interest has been created.** [NYSenate.gov](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/UCC/8-511?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

### Where the research stands

We've established three important propositions:

- **DTC has express authority under Rule 4(A) to repledge qualifying participant collateral to lenders.**
- **Federal Reserve lending documentation contains a mechanism for obtaining enforceable security interests in collateral.**
- **NY UCC 8-511(c) can prioritize a clearing corporation's secured creditor over its entitlement holders if a securities shortfall occurs.**

What we have **not** established is that the Federal Reserve has actually obtained such an interest from DTC pursuant to Title VIII.

The most productive next step is obtaining the **DTC–Federal Reserve lending documentation, if any, governing Title VIII advances**, or determining whether the Federal Reserve would instead require a specially negotiated collateral agreement.

That would connect the two independently documented mechanisms and show whether the Federal Reserve could actually become the secured creditor contemplated by Rule 4(A) and NY UCC 8-511(c).
