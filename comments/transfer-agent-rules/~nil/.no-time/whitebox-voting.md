## Confidential proxy voting evidence

Roberta Romano, *Does Confidential Proxy Voting Matter?*, NBER Working Paper No. 9126 (Sept. 2002), https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w9126/w9126.pdf. Studying shareholder and management proposals at 130 firms that adopted confidential voting, Romano finds no statistically significant change in voting outcomes or firm performance attributable to the reform (abstract; pp. 32–33). The argument for secrecy was that issuer management could pressure institutional investors with business relationships, but the data did not support the predicted shift in their votes (pp. 2–4, 32–33). For the eventual on-chain proxy-voting discussion, distinguish **confidentiality of individual votes** from **independently verifiable tabulation**: an absence of measurable aggregate voting changes does not by itself resolve the procedural case for protecting voters.
