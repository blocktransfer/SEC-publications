# Historical bank nominee / street names

Historical background for the transfer-agent proposal. These names are generally **nominee or street-name entities used by banks and custodians**, not separate central securities depositories like DTC. The distinction matters when comparing them with Cede & Co.

## Examples

| Bank / custodian | Nominee / street names found in government sources |
| --- | --- |
| Bank of New York / BNY Mellon | **Lerche & Co.**; later **Hare & Co.** and **Charles Frederic & Co.** |
| Chase Manhattan Bank | **Kane & Co.** |
| Morgan Guaranty Trust Co. | **Carson & Co.**, **Reing & Co.**, **Kelly & Co.**, **Genoy & Co.**; the 1971 nominee list also includes **Powers & Co.** and **Tegge & Co.**, among many others |
| Chemical Bank | **C. A. England & Co.**; **J. C. Orr & Co.** |
| Manufacturers Hanover Trust Co. | **Sigler & Co.** |
| First National City Bank | **King & Co.** |
| Mellon Bank / Mellon Bank & Trust | **Pace & Co.**; later records also show **Mac & Co.** and **Bost & Co.** |

## Government-source trail

- **2 July 1970 Congressional Record, Extensions of Remarks, p. 22620.** In a footnote explaining Penn Central stock ownership, the Record says commercial banks held shares through bank nominees and maps Morgan Guaranty to Carson, Reing, Kelly, and Genoy; Chemical Bank to C. A. England and J. C. Orr; Bank of New York to Lerche; Manufacturers Hanover to Sigler; Chase Manhattan to Kane; and First National City to King.  
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1970-pt17/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1970-pt17-2-2.pdf

- **24 June 1971 Congressional Record, Extensions of Remarks.** Senator Lee Metcalf inserted the American Society of Corporate Secretaries' *Nominee List* into the Congressional Record. The list maps nominee names to the institutions behind them. Its Morgan Guaranty entry is especially extensive and includes Carson, Genoy, Kelly, Powers, Reing, Shaw, Tegge, and many additional names.  
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1971-pt17/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1971-pt17-1-3.pdf

- **12 October 1971 Congressional Record, Senate, p. 35691.** Metcalf explains the opacity directly: Carson, Powers, and Tegge were Morgan Guaranty names; **Pace & Co.** was Mellon Bank & Trust; and **Cede & Co.** was identified with the Stock Clearing Corporation, then a wholly owned NYSE subsidiary. This is a useful contemporaneous comparison between bank nominees and the predecessor clearing/depository structure associated with Cede.  
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GPO-CRECB-1971-pt27/pdf/GPO-CRECB-1971-pt27-5-1.pdf

- **Federal court record (2008).** A district-court filing identifies **Hare & Co.** as the nominee for Bank of New York Mellon Corporation.  
  https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ncmd-1_08-cv-00225/pdf/USCOURTS-ncmd-1_08-cv-00225-0.pdf

- **SEC filings — later BNY Mellon/Mellon usage.** SEC-filed records identify **Charles Frederic & Co.** as a nominee for The Bank of New York Mellon, **Mac & Co.** in connection with Mellon Bank/BNY Mellon custody arrangements, and **Bost & Co.** c/o Mellon Bank or BNY Mellon.  
  https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1725472/000119312526187221/d52285d486bpos.htm  
  https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/31791/000119312509112853/dex1018.htm  
  https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/30146/000080488713000004/group110sai-3132.htm

## Why flag it

- A registered holder name such as **Lerche & Co.**, **Kane & Co.**, or **Carson & Co.** could conceal the bank or trust institution acting for underlying customers or beneficiaries.
- The same institution could use multiple nominee names simultaneously. The 1971 Morgan Guaranty list is an unusually clear example.
- **Cede & Co. is related but structurally different.** The historical Congressional Record itself identifies Cede with the securities-clearing infrastructure, whereas the other names above were bank nominee/street names. Do not collapse the two categories when discussing registered ownership, depositories, custodians, or transfer-agent records.
- The historical sources may be useful when analyzing whether modern tokenized or omnibus structures recreate older layers of nominee registration under new terminology.

### Source-text caution

OCR of the 1970 Congressional Record sometimes renders Morgan Guaranty's **Genoy & Co.** as “Geney & Co.” The 24 June 1971 nominee list itself gives **Genoy & Co.**
