SR-DTC-2026-007 is a 2026 rule filing by the Depository Trust Company (DTC) that would overhaul how transfer agents interact with DTC when processing tender offers, rights subscriptions, and other corporate actions.

The important part: the SEC suspended the rule on July 9, 2026, after the Securities Transfer Association (STA) objected.

DTC argues that many contested requirements already existed. STA counters that incorporating them into a new master agreement creates enforceable obligations, potentially shifting liability to transfer agents and exceeding their authority to bind issuers.


## What DTC proposed

DTC filed the proposal on May 11, 2026, seeking to amend its Operational Arrangements.

The changes would:

1. Combine the separate ATOP (Automated Tender Offer Program) and ASOP (Automated Subscription Offer Program) agent designations into one.
2. Replace the separate master agreements with a single agreement covering both programs.
3. Consolidate and update the operating procedures for participating agents.
4. Establish requirements concerning electronic instructions, deadlines, agent responsibilities, and corporate-action processing.&#x20;


The STA argued that DTC was doing considerably more than simplifying paperwork.

Its objections included:

- Authority: Transfer agents could be required to make contractual representations on behalf of issuers without authorization.
- Investor equality: DTC participants could receive favorable treatment compared with directly registered shareholders, particularly regarding submission deadlines.
- Liability: The new agreement could transfer additional operational and legal risks onto transfer agents.
- Deadlines: Some procedures, including a 30-minute withdrawal-processing requirement, could be impractical.
- DTC discretion: DTC could gain substantial control over aspects of corporate-action processing.

The SEC considered these concerns sufficiently serious to suspend the immediately effective filing and begin formal proceedings under Section 19(b)(3)(C) of the Exchange Act. This was not a final finding that the proposal violated the law.

Suspended — no final decision located

The SEC's docket still lists the July 9 suspension as its latest formal action.
