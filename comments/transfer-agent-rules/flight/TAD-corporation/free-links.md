# Free links and interfaces

## NSCC registration condition — 42 FR 3916, 3938

**SEC release / file:** Exchange Act Release No. **34-13163** (Jan. 13, 1977), **File No. 600-15**

The SEC conditioned NSCC's integrated operation on interconnection with the regional clearing agencies. It required NSCC to establish **full interfaces** with Midwest Clearing Corporation, Pacific Clearing Corporation, and Stock Clearing Corporation of Philadelphia, and **appropriate links** with Boston Stock Exchange Clearing Corporation and TAD Depository Corporation.

The order also required NSCC to offer to operate each interface or link **without charging the other clearing agency for interface movements** and without imposing an **interface fee on participants**.

This is the key source for the original free-linking requirement.

Source: **42 FR 3916, 3938 (Jan. 21, 1977)**  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1977-01-21/pdf/FR-1977-01-21.pdf

## TAD-specific implementation — 42 FR 25568

By May 1977, the SEC said NSCC had met with TAD Depository Corporation and BSECC, developed an approach, and would shortly provide each organization a proposed method of linking for review.

Source: **42 FR 25568 (May 18, 1977)**  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1977-05-18/pdf/FR-1977-05-18.pdf

## National-system hearing question — 43 FR 16231

**SEC release:** **34-14648**

In its 1978 national clearance-and-settlement hearings, the Commission specifically asked whether each registered clearing agency, when requested by another registered clearing agency, should be required to establish an interface and **offer to operate that interface for free**.

Footnote 5 makes the scope unusually broad: clearing corporation-to-clearing corporation, depository-to-depository, and depository-to-clearing corporation interfaces.

Source: **43 FR 16231 (Apr. 17, 1978)**  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1978-04-17/pdf/FR-1978-04-17.pdf

## Free as to participants — 45 FR 80224

**SEC release:** **34-17343**

In the BSPS/NSCC municipal-securities interface order, the Commission required the interface to be **free as to participants**. A participant using the interface was not supposed to pay more than it would for a comparable transaction handled within a single clearing agency.

The order also discusses the policy of mutualizing interface costs across each clearing agency's participant base rather than imposing a separate interface toll.

Source: **45 FR 80224 (Dec. 3, 1980)**  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1980-12-03/pdf/FR-1980-12-03.pdf

## Interface-cost mutualization — 47 FR 28512

**SEC release / files:** **34-18823**, **SR-MCC-82-8**, **SR-MSTC-82-11**

The Commission stated that it generally had required interface costs incurred by each interfacing clearing agency to be **absorbed by that clearing agency and mutualized across its own participant base**.

This is the authority later cited in footnote 28 of Release 34-19705 when the SEC described free interfaces as a central characteristic of the National System.

Source: **47 FR 28512 (June 30, 1982)**  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1982-06-30/pdf/FR-1982-06-30.pdf

## Later SEC explanation — 48 FR 20189

**SEC release / file:** **34-19705**, **SR-NSCC-82-28**

In the 1983 NSCC Phase II order, the SEC described the continuing requirement as a system-level policy, stating that NSCC remained required to provide **free interfaces to other registered clearing agencies** under the 1977 registration order.

The Commission cited Release No. 18823, **47 FR 28512 (June 30, 1982)**, for the principle that interface costs generally should be absorbed by each interfacing clearing agency and mutualized across its own participant base.

The SEC then characterized the free-interface requirement as a **central characteristic of the National System**.

The same discussion notes that some conditions had become obsolete, specifically that NSCC could no longer establish the TAD link because TAD had ceased operations and its registration had been terminated.

Source: **48 FR 20189 (May 4, 1983), Release No. 34-19705, File No. SR-NSCC-82-28**  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1983-05-04/pdf/FR-1983-05-04.pdf

## Full and free interfaces — 48 FR 55654

**SEC release / file:** **34-20461**, **SR-DTC-77-10**

The Commission gave a detailed explanation of its **full and free interface** policy. It explained that interfacing clearing corporations should not charge each other for interface movements and that participants should not face either identified interface fees or charges functioning as hidden interface fees.

The release also explains an important limit: a reciprocal interface can be subject to a no-charge policy while a distinct **linked service**, where one clearing agency actually performs a separate service for another, may be chargeable.

Source: **48 FR 55654 (Dec. 14, 1983)**  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1983-12-14/pdf/FR-1983-12-14.pdf

## Later DTC no-charge policy — 61 FR 4807–08

**SEC release / file:** **34-36799**, **SR-DTC-94-16**

The SEC later approved a DTC interdepository policy stating that, absent another agreement, DTC **shall not charge** another registered depository for reciprocal book-entry delivery services and will not pay for the other depository's reciprocal book-entry delivery services.

The policy preserves charges for physical-certificate handling and separately defined linked services, reinforcing the distinction between a free reciprocal interface and other services.

Source: **61 FR 4807–08 (Feb. 8, 1996)**  
https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-1996-02-08/pdf/96-2701.pdf

## Why this matters for TAD

TAD's temporary-registration status did **not** itself bar interconnection. The SEC's 1977 NSCC registration order affirmatively required a free link with TAD, and the later 1983 order treated the disappearance of that link as a consequence of TAD's shutdown and deregistration rather than its temporary status.

Taken together, the releases show a recurring Commission policy that reciprocal core clearing and depository interfaces should not be burdened by tolls that disadvantage transactions crossing between clearing agencies. The SEC instead repeatedly discussed absorbing or mutualizing those interface costs within the participating clearing agencies.
