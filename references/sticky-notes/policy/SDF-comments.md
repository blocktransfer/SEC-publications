#### SA: 
The Stellar Development Foundation (SDF) is active in regulatory consultations beyond its U.S. FinCEN comment letters.  Recent filings show that SDF has supplied responses to U.S. and foreign government agencies and regulators on topics such as digital‑asset research, financial inclusion and environmental sustainability.  Below are the major additional comment letters that could be located.

**U.S. Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP – March 3 2023)** – As part of President Biden’s Executive Order 14067 on the “responsible development of digital assets,” the OSTP asked for public input on its digital‑asset research agenda.  SDF’s response emphasises two priorities: (1) the federal government should promote *interoperability* by developing technical standards that allow various digital‑asset systems to interact freely; and (2) agencies should research *programmable* digital assets to explore how features like conditional transfers, escrow‑like functions and instant swaps could be used in future government programs.  SDF explained that the Stellar network is an open‑source, fast and scalable infrastructure supporting more than seven million accounts and over ten billion operations.  The letter encourages OSTP to ensure interoperability across both digital‑asset platforms and between digital assets and the traditional financial system.

**U.S. Department of the Treasury – Request for Information on Financial Inclusion (Feb. 20 2024)** – The Treasury’s Office of Consumer Policy sought input for a national strategy on financial inclusion.  SDF’s response argues that financial inclusion hinges on *access* to basic financial services and that digital wallets and open‑source, interoperable blockchain technology can lower barriers for underserved communities.  SDF asks Treasury to define financial inclusion in terms of the products and services consumers should be able to access and suggests actions such as expanding low‑cost digital‑transaction services and separating anti‑money‑laundering compliance from financial disadvantage.  The letter notes that the Stellar network supports over seven million accounts and processed more than 14 billion operations by December 2023, and emphasises that SDF is committed to working with lawmakers and regulators to promote open systems.

**European Securities and Markets Authority (ESMA – Nov. 9 2023)** – ESMA requested comments on environmental‑sustainability indicators for blockchain networks as part of its rulemaking under the Markets in Crypto‑Assets (MiCA) regulation.  SDF’s submission urges ESMA to adopt a unified methodology for measuring the sustainability impacts of consensus mechanisms.  SDF explains that the Stellar network’s “proof‑of‑agreement” consensus uses far less energy than proof‑of‑work or proof‑of‑stake systems and cites a PwC‑developed Blockchain Sustainability Framework showing that Stellar’s annual energy use is roughly 481 MWh, or 0.00032 kWh per transaction.  The letter highlights the network’s interoperability with traditional finance—such as the MoneyGram Access on/off‑ramp allowing people to convert between cash and digital assets without a bank account—and urges regulators to consider social and governance impacts alongside environmental factors.

**Financial Stability Board (FSB – Sept. 2022)** – The FSB sought feedback on regulatory approaches to crypto‑asset activities.  SDF’s response argues that well‑designed stablecoins can lower cross‑border payment costs and support financial inclusion, especially in developing economies.  The letter notes that cross‑border payments often cost 6–7 % of the transaction value, whereas blockchain‑based stablecoins can deliver near‑instant transfers at significantly lower cost.  SDF urges regulators to tailor rules that leverage the transparency of blockchain rather than replicate the burdens of the fiat‑currency reporting regime.

No evidence was found of SDF submitting standalone comment letters to the U.S. SEC, CFTC, OCC or CFPB during the 2023–2024 period.  In addition to the above filings, SDF occasionally signs joint industry letters—such as the FIT21 Coalition letter to U.S. House leaders (supporting H.R. 4763) and participates in associations like the Blockchain Association and Chamber of Digital Commerce—but these are coalition statements rather than individual agency comments.
