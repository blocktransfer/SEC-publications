## Message

There is one firm called Securitize that is also an SEC-registered stock
transfer agent. Information about their company and underlying 'blockchain
transfer agent' service (according to their website) is scarce at best. It
seems the company focuses mainly on private fund-admin and cap-table
services. I had significant difficulties finding any of the decentralized
tech they're using, despite the blockchain buzzwords all over their site.
The CEO has a lot of experience in VC, according to LinkedIn, and the Firm
itself claims to have support from large institutional banks. They act as
more of a centralized entity, from what I can tell... essentially
functioning like the Computershares of the world.

3 of the 7 patents listed below were abandoned, as it is complex to patent
open-source blockchain technology.

US20190197622A1 - This is a vague way of using blockchain to record
CUSIP-related information through digital tokens (very similar to what
we're doing). The patent was abandoned. Due to vagueness, the patent could
be applied to almost all securities recordkeeping.

US20190139136A1 - This is a vague way of using blockchain for security
clearing, similar settlement services, and subsequent asset lending (among
other things). It discusses the open auditability enabled by blockchain
tech which streamlines tax accounting and public transparency of trades.
The patent was abandoned. This patent, though extremely vague, relates more
to decentralized asset management and less to a decentralized stock
transfer agent.

JP6697008B2 - This patent isn't in English, but it talks about federated
implementations of sidechain verification for stocks, commodities,
currencies, and derivatives, which we are using in some parts of our code.
The patent discusses settlement risk in forex markets and proposes ways to
streamline asset authorization and transfer to enable decentralized
trading. Translation can be found on Google patents. The patent rarely
talks about equities or transfer and settlement of digital shares (what
we're doing).

US10833843B1 - This patent discusses means to manage blockchain access
through metadata information. There are also systems described for
interacting with a blockchain for asset management based on a private
ledger. I've read this patent a few times and legitimately have no clue how
it was granted. The sixth page is a description of blockchain, and the
remaining 14 pages just talk about storing private keys and accessing them
securely. This patent encompasses every blockchain application on Earth,
and I see no court deliverability or enforceability possible.

US20190385155A1 - This patent discusses trading tokenized financial
instruments through smart contracts (very similar to what we're doing).
Furthermore, the patent talks about reporting decentralized settlement on
the blockchain to issuers, tax authorities, and regulatory agents (very
similar to what we're doing). The patent 'Summary of the Invention' section
focuses on the primary issuance of securities and subsequent store of value
on blockchains. However, once you get down to the 'Detailed Description of
the Invention,' the information presented becomes significantly vaguer. By
the logic in the patent, almost any decentralized token that stores value
(essentially all of them) are embodied under the invention. The patent even
goes so far as to discuss credit cards based on digital tokens (this is
very common in the industry and many firms compete in this space).
Furthermore, the patent tries to cover 32-bit order-type encoding which has
been standardized in the market for decades. Because of other significant
vagueness in the pending patent, I highly doubt it will be granted.

US20190130483A1 - This patent discusses issuing asset-back securities
through blockchain tokens. The primary 'invention' cited are using 'mint,'
a common Solidity function call, to establish decentralized assets. The
patent further discusses the transfer of assets after issuance through a
decentralized ledger. Lastly, the patent discusses the redemption of tokens
for the underlying assets. Again, I have no clue how this patent got
granted... It essentially covers every single ERC20 token on Earth. Just
off the top of my head, the Coinbase-Circle USDC, Gemini USDG, Zhao's
Tether, etc. encompass over 40B and heavily rely on every feature detailed
in this patent. Anyway, this is a very general technological patent that
doesn't particularly talk about stock transfer agents in any way whatsoever.

US20190080407A1 - An older revision of US20190197622A1 that discusses the
implementation of CUSIP-tokenized securities on ATS and its implications.
The patent was abandoned. Very pertinent under Exchange Act Rule 3a1-1(a),
but this has essentially nothing to do with issuance or settlement under
stock transfer agents.
