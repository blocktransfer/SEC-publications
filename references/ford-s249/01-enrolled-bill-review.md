# S. 249 case file (1) — enrolled-bill review and agency recommendations

**Ford Library file:** Box 25, folder “6/4/75 S249 Securities Act Amendments of 1975 (1)”  
**PDF:** https://www.fordlibrarymuseum.gov/sites/default/files/pdf_documents/library/document/0055/1669000.pdf

## What's in the file

This is the substantive enrolled-bill review file. It includes:

- OMB's memorandum to the President summarizing S. 249 and the agency recommendations.
- Jim Cannon's recommendation that the President sign the bill and approve the signing statement.
- Treasury's enrolled-bill analysis.
- SEC Chairman Ray Garrett Jr.'s recommendation.
- Justice, Council of Economic Advisers, Federal Home Loan Bank Board, and Labor views.
- A recommended presidential signing message.
- June 2-3 materials concerning the unintended state/local stock-transfer-tax language and planned corrective legislation.

## Key facts

- OMB describes S. 249 as the product of roughly ten years of Executive Branch and congressional work to modernize securities regulation (scan p. 2).
- The national market system was not described as a single federal trading venue. OMB says the SEC was to work with industry to facilitate an interconnected system, relying substantially on competition and self-regulation while strengthening federal oversight to make sure essential system components were actually implemented (scan pp. 3-4).
- OMB identifies interoperability as an affirmative federal objective: the SEC was expected to make exchange and other information systems compatible and to ensure brokers and dealers could obtain the information necessary to complete transactions (scan p. 4).
- OMB says the SEC would receive general regulatory authority over the securities-handling system, including clearing agencies, depositories, and transfer agents, while bank regulators retained inspection roles for banking institutions (scan p. 4).
- Treasury describes a national market system in which competition among market makers would increase depth and liquidity, and says the SEC would have overall policy and rulemaking responsibility for development and coordination of the national clearance system (scan pp. 11-12).
- The SEC endorsed the bill even though some provisions differed from its own recommendations. Garrett characterized it as an important accomplishment in federal securities regulation (scan pp. 17-19).
- The Council of Economic Advisers supported signature but recorded a reservation that broader SEC discretion could, if used poorly, work against the statute's pro-competition purpose (scan p. 22).
- The file documents a late-discovered drafting error affecting state and local taxation where a transfer agent supplied the jurisdictional hook. Congressional sponsors and Treasury expected prompt corrective legislation (scan pp. 34-38).

## Key quotable passages

- OMB: “strengthen SEC's oversight and regulatory powers” (scan p. 3).
- OMB: systems should be “compatible with each other” (scan p. 4).
- Treasury: “competitive forces would be accorded the widest latitude” (scan p. 11).

## Best uses

This is the strongest executive-branch source for explaining the mechanism behind the 1975 reforms: federal supervision and rulemaking were paired with competition, self-regulation, compatible market-information systems, and coordinated clearing and settlement rather than simply preserving preexisting exchange structures.
