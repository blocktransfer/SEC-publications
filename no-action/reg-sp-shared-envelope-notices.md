## Relevant facts and proposed practice

The request concerns [identify the particular incident and the specific affected individuals or recipient group without including their sensitive customer information in a publicly released version]. Before submitting this request, the Agent will verify the number of recipients, the mailing address they each designated, and whether any recipient requires separate or confidential delivery.

For the specified mailing, the Agent proposes to:

1. Determine which natural persons are entitled to notice under 17 CFR 248.30(a)(4), without treating co-residence, family relationships, or joint ownership as grounds to omit any individual.
2. Prepare a **separate, complete, individually named written notice** for each affected individual, containing all information required by 17 CFR 248.30(a)(4)(iv).
3. Use a common outer envelope **only** where each addressee has designated the same current mailing address and there is no indication that joint delivery would compromise reasonable receipt or confidentiality. The outer envelope would name each intended recipient, and each person's notice would be placed in a separately addressed, sealed inner envelope.
4. Mail the package by a reasonably reliable postal method, retain records showing every individual for whom a notice was enclosed, and respond appropriately to any returned or undeliverable mailing.
5. Send separate outer mailings whenever an individual has requested separate delivery, an address is uncertain, a relevant confidentiality concern exists, or applicable law imposes a more protective requirement.

The Agent would not send one generic notice in place of multiple individual notices. Nor would it assume that delivery of one person's notice satisfies the obligation to notify another.

## Applicable provision and analysis

Rule 248.30(a)(4)(i) requires notice to *each affected individual* and transmission by a means designed so that each can reasonably be expected to receive actual notice in writing. The text speaks to the individual recipient, the contents of the notice, and the reasonable expectation of receipt. It does not expressly prescribe a separate outer envelope or separate postage for every individual.

In adopting the amendments, the Commission emphasized individually directed written notice, including circumstances in which a covered institution would send individual notices by mail rather than rely on an electronic method lacking the requisite consent. The adopting release did not expressly address the narrower question whether **separate individual notices** may be enclosed in **one shared outer mailing**. See *Regulation S-P: Privacy of Consumer Financial Information and Safeguarding Customer Information*, Exchange Act Release No. 100155, 89 FR 47688 (June 3, 2024).

The Agent submits that an individually prepared and addressed notice does not cease to be individual merely because its outer envelope also carries a second person's separately addressed notice. The separate sealed inner envelopes, common verified address, and delivery safeguards described above are intended to preserve each individual's reasonable expectation of receiving the notice. Conversely, an undifferentiated household notice presents a different question and is not the practice for which relief is requested.

## Specific request

On the particular facts and representations supplied above, would the Staff agree that the proposed mailing method satisfies the written-delivery requirement of 17 CFR 248.30(a)(4)(i)? Alternatively, would the Staff decline to recommend enforcement action **solely on the basis of the shared outer envelope**, where each affected individual is separately named and receives a complete, individually addressed notice within a sealed inner envelope?

This request does not seek relief from the notification trigger, the timing or content requirements of Regulation S-P, any applicable state-law notice requirements, or the Agent's obligation to select a method reasonably designed for actual written notice to every affected individual. The Agent recognizes that Staff guidance is not a Commission rule or order.
